I would like to try another way of finding a noise mask that matches a given sample.
Add a new option --mask-method [dft|markov] to allow the new method to be chosen and 
add new files markov.cpp and markov.h.
I would like to use a markov model to generate a matching texture ( perhaps it is a markov
random field, not sure) The idea is  that the neighbourhood of a pixel constrains what
values that pixel is likely to take ( it's distribution )  

We will consider a small
neighbourhood of perhaps +-5 pixels. Perhaps a larger neighbourhood is needed but
the closest neighbors are likely to be most influential. In addition we will consider pairs
of neighbours instead of single pixels. Some pairs that are likely to be more influential
than others  are  ((x-2,y),(x-1,y)) and ((x-1,y),(x+1,y)) and their symmetries or
((x-1,y-1),(x+1,y+1)).  Far neighbours are likely needed because the +-2 neighbourhood
is likely too small to fully reproduce grain structure.

For each pair e.g. ((x-2,y),(x-1,y), we iterate over the texture sample, and build tables
of the quantised neighbours img[x-2,y]/10,img[x-1,y]/10 of the set img[x,y]
  (e.g. table_{img[x-2,y]}_{img[x-1,y]}.add( += img[x,y])  

With a neighborhood of +-5 you can about 11^4 tables, so you need some way to eliminate 
uninteresting pairs. A pair is uninteresting if its values are not correlated with
the central pixel, iow  the distribution it collects is about the overall pixel value
distribution.

Perhaps the attention mechanism can be used to eliminate uninteresting pairs, and 
to combine pairs that are interesting.
