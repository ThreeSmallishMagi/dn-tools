A command-line renderer for black-and-white output

Every tone you have, from two you can print.

A dithering engine that works in linear light from end to end, and builds its threshold masks by learning the grain of a real film scan — so the texture that carries the tone belongs to a photograph rather than to an algorithm.

What is new if you have used the first version

The first version dithered with round, random or blue-noise thresholds. This one adds film grain, and the means to choose it well. New to dither? Skip this list; the rest of the page starts from the beginning.

  • Film grain as the threshold. Four generators learn a mask from a film scan — dft, markov, inpaint and direct — chosen with --mask-method and fed with --mask-sample. Blue noise is still the default.
  • A film library. install_dither installs scans of six films, cuts each a grain-only crop and calibrates them, so --mask-sample Kodak-TriX works with no further setup.
  • --supersample and --out-size, to put the grain below the picture's detail and choose the output size.
  • --mask-scale for coarser or finer grain, and --level-spacing device for a panel with its own levels.
  • --explain, --set, --budget and --mask-calibrate: every default says where it came from, and built masks are cached.
  • maketexture, a companion tool that synthesises grain from a patch.
  • --threshold blue is now --threshold mask, since blue noise is one mask among five. The old spelling still works.

The shape of a run

Decode, dither, encode — and never guess in between

Every image is decoded to linear light before anything touches it, and re-encoded only on the way out. The curve is applied exactly at those two boundaries and nowhere inside the loop, which is what lets a half-lit pixel average to the light it should emit rather than to the pixel value that looks like it.

Input may be 8- or 16-bit. The difference is not tidiness: an 8-bit target cannot express anything below one output level, so error diffusion and the multi-frame budget have nothing left to distribute. On a gradient spanning four 8-bit codes, more exposures buy exactly nothing from an 8-bit source and a great deal from a 16-bit one.

--threshold

Three ways to decide a pixel

The threshold rule and the error diffusion are independent axes: every rule works at any diffusion strength. Shown here with diffusion off, so each rule stands alone.

dither dog.png --threshold mask --spatial 0 --out out.png

--spatial

How much of the error is handed onward

Error diffusion is serpentine Floyd–Steinberg — even rows left to right, odd rows right to left, so the error is always pushed towards pixels not yet visited and the directional worming of a raster scan never appears. --spatial scales how much of the accumulated error the threshold rule sees, not how much is diffused; the residual is always passed on in full, which is what keeps the output averaging to the target at any setting. In a --frames run it applies to each pixel's total over the frames as well as to each frame.

Why it is the fraction the rule sees. Scaling what is passed on instead would discard part of every residual. With a mask those residuals are not zero-mean — the mask is a fixed function of position, so it correlates with the error field feeding back into it — and at --spatial 0.5 discarding them lifts the shadows by 27/1000 and crushes the highlights by 19/1000.

--levels · --level-spacing

More rungs on the ladder

Two levels is the default and the hardest case. Up to 256 are available, and where the rungs sit is a separate question from how many there are.

Three ladders, because there are three displays. srgb puts the rungs at even 8-bit pixel values — right when the file is the display signal. linear puts them at even steps of light. device puts them where a panel with exactly --levels of its own would put them, which is the right answer for a 3-bit mono screen and differs from srgb by up to 2% of a rung whenever levels-1 does not divide 255.

--mask-method

Five ways to build a threshold mask

A mask is a tile of thresholds, flat in histogram and tiled toroidally across the image. What it looks like decides what the dithering looks like, and these five disagree completely about how to make one. Four are periodic by construction; the fifth is a crop of a photograph and has to earn its join. markov and inpaint grow structure sweep by sweep, and after every sweep their power spectrum is matched to the film's in 16 frequency bands, so it stays at the grain's scale.

Lower is better everywhere. grain asks whether the shape of a mask's grain, block by block, could have come from the film, scored against the scan as it is and in units of how much one real patch differs from another — so 1 is indistinguishable. spectrum is the RMS log ratio of radial power against the film high-passed at 32 px. No generator wins every column: dft owns the spectrum by construction, direct is the film itself, and markov and inpaint trade the spectrum for local structure. Masks as installed and calibrated, 256².

--mask-sample

Learning the grain of an actual film

Point --mask-sample at a scan and the mask is built to resemble it. Point it at a directory of scans, or name a film in the library at ~/.config/dither/samples/, and every scan becomes data — because a film is not one frame, and a model fitted to a single exposure learns that frame's density as much as the emulsion's character. Each film also has a crop at ~/.config/dither/crops/, a window chosen to hold grain and nothing else: it is what dft and direct build from, and what the others are held to. Name a sample-based method with no sample and you get Kodak 4X.

A mask lights the sample's brightest grain first, so the dither shows the grain as it is in the scan.

dither dog.png --mask-method inpaint --mask-sample Kodak-4X --supersample 4 # a film name resolves in the library; a path you type always wins over it

--mask-scale

The same grain, coarser

Grain belongs to a format, not to a file. --mask-scale asks for the sample's texture at a different size — for dft as a pure frequency remap, which is why frequencies past the sample's Nyquist are set to zero rather than wrapped. Wrapped, at scale 2 the main lobe would mirror back as a second lobe carrying 15.8% of peak power — fine noise on grain that had been asked to be coarse.

--supersample · --out-size

Dither finer than the picture

Film's look needs grain below the detail, not a finer mask — scaling the mask target finer just converges on white noise. --supersample n dithers on an n-times grid and averages back down in linear light, giving n²+1 tones per output pixel and letting the grain sit where it belongs.

One resample, not two. With --supersample the dither grid is the only place the sub-pixel information exists, so --out-size is reached from there directly rather than via the input's size. Shrinking a 4096 grid to 512 measures 21.1/1000 linear against the truth in one step and 36.4 through 1024 — and the two-step path comes back with more distinct tones, 206 against 64, which is interpolation inventing values rather than detail surviving.

--frames

Spending the error over time

For a display that can show a sequence, the tone can be spread across frames instead of across pixels. Each pixel is given a budget — goal = target × frames — and every frame aims at what is left divided by the frames remaining, so the total converges exactly on the final frame. No separate accumulator, and stochastic thresholds need no special case.

These are previews: the average is re-encoded to 8 bits, so it understates what a display showing the frames in sequence actually emits. --save-frames writes each one separately — measure those, not the average, when the sequence is the product.

maketexture

The check no statistic can make

The companion tool synthesises a texture from a patch using the same generators, and --paste writes the input back with that region replaced. If the synthesis works, the region cannot be found. Below is a 256×256 patch of Kodak 4X replaced entirely by synthesised grain — press the button to see where.

maketexture ~/.config/dither/crops/Kodak-4X.png --sample 256x256+128+128 --paste pasted.png

--explain · --set · --budget · --mask-calibrate

Defaults that say where they came from

Most parameters are not constants but rules, resolved once and recorded with what they depended on. --explain prints the whole resolution, and the origin column is the point of it: a value you gave is never recomputed, and a value the program chose says what it chose from.

$ dither dog.png --mask-method markov --mask-sample Kodak-4X \ --supersample 4 --explain parameter value origin derived from image.width 256 measured supersample 4 user mask.method markov user mask.sample Kodak-4X user threshold mask implied mask.highpass 0 derived mask.method markov.temp 0.645359 measured markov.weights ml measured markov.radius 3 measured mask.size.w 256 derived supersample, image.height, image.width, mask.method, markov.max_repeats, mask.highpass, mask.scale, markov.max_size, budget

The threshold line is reconcile at work. Naming a mask option is a request for a mask, so the threshold default moves to accommodate it rather than leaving your option silently ignored. What cannot be rescued is refused outright, naming both sides — because an option that had no effect alongside a zero exit status is the worst of the available outcomes.

--budget 30s picks the mask size and pair count to fit the time, using per-generator work formulas divided by a throughput measured from real builds. --mask-calibrate measures a generator's awkward parameters against your sample instead of trusting a default fitted on someone else's film — and declines, rather than inventing a number, when the search runs to the end of its bracket without turning over. The answer is kept in the config and every later run on that sample uses it: the three measured lines above came from the calibration made when Kodak 4X was installed. --no-config goes back to the rules.

Masks are cached under a key walked out of the parameter registry, so a repeat is byte-identical to a build and arrives in 0.06s instead of 4.70s. The key hashes the sample's contents rather than its path, and the executable's identity too, so rebuilding a generator invalidates every mask it ever made.
